# Route: Engineering Leadership

Treat management as the design and care of a human system. Start from the
outcome, constraints, observed work, and affected people. Do not explain every
problem as individual performance or reach for a reorganization before testing
smaller changes to clarity, information flow, decision rights, and capacity.

Keep these distinctions explicit:

- accountability is not surveillance;
- delegation transfers a bounded decision, not merely more tasks;
- coaching develops judgment, while direction resolves an urgent or constrained
  choice;
- autonomy needs context, authority, and feedback;
- a meeting is a tool with a purpose, not inherently good or bad;
- team boundaries should follow durable interaction needs, not a fashionable
  diagram.

## Workflow

### 1. Frame the management decision

Name the affected group, current outcome, desired outcome, decision owner,
deadline, and non-negotiable constraints. Reframe vague requests such as "fix
accountability" into observable behavior and consequences. Establish what the
requester may change and what needs consent, executive sponsorship, People/HR
ownership, or specialist review.

### 2. Build an evidence map

Separate direct observations, metrics, recurring reports, isolated anecdotes,
commitments, and assumptions. Seek the work as experienced by contributors as
well as managers, and preserve disagreement and missing voices. Protect
confidentiality; report themes at the smallest necessary level. Do not invent
employee intent, private feedback, health information, team sentiment,
performance evidence, or organizational authority.

### 3. Trace the system

Map the shortest relevant chain from intent to decision to work to feedback.
Record unclear owners, approval waits, dependency queues, duplicated decisions,
interruptions, overloaded specialists, incentives, and missing customer or
operational signals. Note who would gain or lose authority, safety, focus,
growth opportunities, or workload.

### 4. Choose the smallest responsible intervention

Prefer a reversible change with a named owner and review date: clarify a
decision right, narrow a role, cancel or redesign one recurring meeting, add a
delegation checkpoint, protect capacity, or establish a cross-functional
working agreement. Escalate to team redesign only when interaction patterns and
load provide a durable driver.

### 5. Define safeguards

State what the intervention must not damage: psychological safety,
accessibility, sustainable workload, product quality, customer access,
professional growth, or required governance. Never recommend covert monitoring,
public ranking, deceptive feedback collection, forced overtime, or retaliation.

### 6. Run and review

Set a leading signal, a short enough learning window, qualitative and
quantitative signals, and a specific review conversation. Compare the result with the prior state, include
unintended effects, and decide to keep, adjust, reverse, or escalate.

## Default Deliverable

Provide:

1. decision and desired outcome;
2. evidence and important unknowns;
3. system diagnosis, including affected people;
4. smallest recommended intervention and alternatives considered;
5. authority, consent, and safeguards;
6. owner, timing, signals, and review decision;
7. specialist handoffs.

## Guardrails

- Do not diagnose a person from second-hand descriptions.
- Do not turn activity, online status, hours, or message volume into a proxy for
  contribution.
- Do not promise confidentiality that the process cannot protect.
- Do not use "empowerment" to transfer accountability without authority,
  information, capacity, or support.
- Do not prescribe a named team topology, ratio, or meeting cadence as a
  universal rule.
- Do not normalize overload as commitment or treat sustained overtime as a
  planning tool.
- Do not advise on employment law, compensation systems, termination procedure,
  medical issues, or crisis response.

  security, or crisis support when those concerns determine the action.

Named handoffs are not bundled prerequisites. If a named owner is unavailable,
preserve the boundary, disclose the gap, and provide only narrow management
guidance supported by the supplied evidence.
