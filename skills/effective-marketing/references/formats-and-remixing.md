# Formats and Remixing

Choose a social format for the job the content must do. Use variants to explore
meaningfully different directions, not to manufacture volume.

## Choose the Format

### Original Post

Use for one observation, position, lesson, story, result, or question that can
stand alone.

Useful shapes:

- result or observation, then the mechanism
- tension or contradiction, then the supported position
- specific moment, then what changed
- common interpretation, then what it misses
- practical lesson, then the condition under which it applies

Lead with genuine specificity or movement. Do not bolt on a sensational opening
that the post cannot repay.

### Reply or Comment

Use for a proportionate contribution to an existing conversation:

- answer the question that was asked
- add one relevant example, constraint, or implementation detail
- agree or disagree with a specific reason
- ask the useful follow-up the source leaves open
- thank or acknowledge the person directly

Do not restate the source, perform expertise, or hide an unsolicited pitch in a
reply.

### Quote Post or Resource Share

Add value beyond the linked source:

1. direct reaction or recommendation
2. concrete reason, mechanism, application, or limitation
3. related experience or resource only when it helps
4. clear source attribution or link

Do not summarize an article the reader can already see. Explain why it matters,
what it changes, how to use it, or what remains unresolved.

### Thread or Series

Use when the reader benefits from sequence, not merely because the first draft
is long.

- Give the series one governing idea.
- Make each part useful and understandable in order.
- Use transitions to advance the argument, process, or story.
- Avoid repeating the opening claim in every part.
- End when the promised explanation is complete.

When the platform supports a single coherent post that serves the reader
better, do not fragment it for artificial engagement.

### Product or Project Post

Show a verified result, change, artifact, or user situation early. Explain the
context, mechanism, tradeoff, limitation, or remaining work that makes the
update credible.

Avoid feature dumping. Turn the update into a concrete scene:

- what problem or constraint existed
- what changed
- how the change works
- what evidence demonstrates it
- what still requires judgment or verification
- where the reader can inspect or try it

Do not turn a resource share or personal reflection into a hidden product pitch.

### Educational Post

Teach one useful distinction, method, checklist, failure mode, or worked
example. Match depth to the reader's existing knowledge. Preserve conditions
and exceptions that keep the advice true.

Prefer a concrete example or mechanism to an abstract list of tips. If the
source requires long setup, route the full artifact to `effective-writing` and
use social content to introduce or extract one valuable part.

### Announcement or Invitation

State what is happening, for whom, why it matters, when or where it applies,
and what the reader can do next. Keep logistics exact and dated. Do not
manufacture urgency, scarcity, popularity, or social proof.

### Visual, Carousel, or Short-Video Concept

Give the media a job the caption alone cannot do:

- demonstrate a change or workflow
- compare before and after
- sequence a method
- show evidence, data, or an artifact
- make a concept spatially or visually understandable

Provide a usable frame or slide sequence, on-screen text, caption role, and
accessibility needs. Route a pure LinkedIn document request to
the LinkedIn posts route.

## Generate Materially Different Directions

When the direction is genuinely open, select a few useful approaches from the
following set:

- **Closest polish:** preserve the user's thought and characteristic wording
  while improving clarity, order, and rhythm.
- **Sharper tension:** lead with the most defensible surprise, contrast, or
  consequence.
- **Conversational:** sound closer to a spoken observation, question, or
  personal exchange.
- **Proof-led:** begin with the artifact, result, example, or constraint.
- **Mechanism-led:** explain why the result happened or how the method works.
- **Use-case-led:** begin with the situation in which the idea becomes useful.
- **Counterpoint:** challenge a common interpretation without inventing a
  controversy.
- **Playful:** use supported wit, specificity, or an unexpected connection
  without turning the speaker into a performed persona.

Keep all variants inside the same factual boundary. Make each option differ in
angle, structure, evidence, rhythm, or intended response. Changing adjectives,
line breaks, or hook punctuation does not create another direction.

Put the strongest option first. Label options only when the label helps the
user compare them; keep ready-to-use copy more prominent than analysis.

## Make Every Part Advance the Thought

Use the second sentence or paragraph to add something the opening did not:

- reason
- mechanism
- use case
- evidence
- condition or limitation
- contrast
- consequence
- question
- conclusion

Cut a sentence that merely restates the hook or renames the topic. Allow
intentional repetition when it creates emphasis or structure, but do not use a
short-line template for the entire post.

## Repurpose a Source

Before adapting an article, talk, release note, case study, or earlier post:

1. Identify the source's governing idea and evidence.
2. Choose the one part that serves the social audience now.
3. Decide the job of each platform version: introduce, teach, discuss,
   demonstrate, invite, or connect.
4. Rebuild each version around that job.
5. Link or attribute the source where useful.
6. Preserve qualifications and do not make the social version stronger than
   the source supports.

One source can produce several posts only when each adds a distinct use,
mechanism, example, audience, or stage of the story. Do not slice paragraphs
into a queue merely to increase output.

## Plan a Series or Calendar

Build a plan from real source capacity instead of filling dates with generic
topics. Give each item:

- governing idea and intended audience
- purpose or desired reader change
- evidence source and unresolved gap
- format and platform job
- relationship to earlier or planned content
- owner, review state, and publication window when supplied

Reuse one source across platforms only when each version has a clear channel
job. Sequence a series so later pieces add proof, mechanism, application,
counterpoint, or learning instead of paraphrasing the first piece.

Choose a cadence the person or team can sustain at the required evidence and
review quality. Do not prescribe a universal frequency, best time, or content
mix as an algorithm guarantee. When learning matters, attach one audience or
message question to each item and review qualitative responses alongside
available platform signals.

## Review the Finished Content

- Does the opening earn attention and repay its promise?
- Is the main point understandable without invented context?
- Does every claim have evidence, attribution, or clear opinion framing?
- Does the speaker sound recognizable without copied signature phrases or
  manufactured quirks?
- Does each paragraph or part move the thought?
- Is the commercial intent honest and proportionate?
- Does the ending complete the piece or offer a relevant next step?
- Are the alternatives materially different?
- Does each platform version feel composed for that platform rather than
  mechanically resized?
