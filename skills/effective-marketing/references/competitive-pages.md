# Competitive and Alternatives Pages

Help a buyer make an informed choice among real alternatives. Use approved
positioning and verified competitor evidence; a page draft cannot supply missing
research or decide a new pricing or migration policy.

## Choose the comparison the reader needs

| Intent | Useful scope |
| --- | --- |
| Alternative to one named product | Why someone might switch, who fits our offer, and consequential differences |
| Several alternatives to a named product | A bounded selection based on buyer criteria, with fit and limits per option |
| Our offer versus one competitor | Comparable facts, decision criteria, strengths, constraints, and next steps |
| Two other products compared | A fair comparison of those products; introduce our offer only if it serves the reader's question |

Choose the format from the reader's decision rather than making all four pages
by default. Explain the selection basis when listing alternatives. Include
manual, in-house, or do-nothing alternatives when they are real candidates.
Do not place our product first or declare a winner merely because we own the
page; recommend by fit and show when another option is stronger.

## Use a consistent fact basis

Receive current profiles from `effective-product` when structured competitor
research is needed. Keep consequential source references and observation dates
with the drafting notes, outside publishable copy when appropriate. If a fact
is unknown or contradictory, omit it, qualify it, or return the research gap.

Compare equivalent units and conditions. Prices may differ by currency,
geography, billing period, seat or usage minimum, tax treatment, plan, add-ons,
and contract. A public entry price is not necessarily a buyer's total cost.
State material limits beside price claims; do not convert missing information
into a zero or a disadvantage for the competitor.

Use the same approved facts across comparison pages, sales material, and pricing
copy. Reference an existing evidence record or maintain one compact fact table
when repeated use warrants it; do not mandate a new folder or data system.
Reopen affected claims when a plan, capability, policy, or source changes.

Distinguish vendor claims, documented behavior, hands-on observations, and
customer reports. A few reviews do not establish prevalence; estimated search
traffic does not establish customer count, revenue, or product quality.

## Build the argument around fit

Explain the reader situation, relevant options, decision criteria, comparable
evidence, and what must be true for a recommendation to hold. A feature table
can orient the reader but rarely explains a consequential trade-off alone.
Show actual competitor strengths, meaningful limits of our offer, and the
audience for whom each alternative is appropriate.

Include switching work when relevant: data export and import, integration
compatibility, retraining, parallel operation, contract commitments, and known
losses or prerequisites. Express approved support and migration terms; do not
promise free migration, savings, compatibility, or a time-to-switch without
evidence and owner agreement.

Choose a proportionate next action: inspect documentation, validate a specific
requirement, try an available plan, or discuss fit. Use real policies and proof
for reassurance. Reject invented testimonials, competitor weaknesses, review
scores, synthetic expert panels, and urgency.

Return the requested draft or brief, consequential evidence gaps, and decisions
the owner must resolve. Page hierarchy, URLs, metadata, internal linking,
structured data, implementation, and disclosures go to `effective-web`; their
technical configuration does not guarantee search visibility.
