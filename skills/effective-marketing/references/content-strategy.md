# Content Strategy

Build a portfolio around audience questions and decisions, not an arbitrary
publishing quota. Search discovery, social sharing, partner distribution, sales
enablement, and direct audience relationships can serve different jobs.

## Establish the evidence and capacity

Use available customer questions, sales objections, support records, product
evidence, search queries, existing assets, channel performance, and distribution
access. Distinguish observed demand from a plausible topic idea. Preserve the
segment and collection limits of the evidence.

Inventory only the assets needed for the decision: audience, question, intent,
format, channel, approved proof, current usefulness, and maintenance needs. If
measurement exists, distinguish impressions, visits, engagement, qualified
inquiries, activation, and commercial outcomes. Missing analytics is a limit,
not permission to fabricate a baseline.

Account for the actual bottleneck: subject expertise, proof, writing, design,
engineering, distribution access, or maintenance. A topic requiring unavailable
evidence or production capacity is not ready merely because its search volume
looks attractive.

## Group topics by intent and audience progress

Start from the question and situation behind a query or proposed piece. Separate
learning a concept, diagnosing a problem, implementing a solution, comparing
options, and choosing an offer. Similar words can express different needs;
different words can express the same one.

When using a keyword export, preserve its date, geography, source, and metric
definitions. Group variants that serve the same intent into one useful asset;
split when the audience, task, or required answer differs. Volumes and difficulty
scores are estimates, not evidence of qualified demand or guaranteed ranking.
Verify current search results when their format or intent matters to a decision.

A cluster is a group of related audience questions with a useful relationship,
not a mandate to create one page per keyword. Map proposed topics to existing
assets first. Distinguish a real gap from a terminology variant, duplicate,
outdated answer, or a topic better handled by product documentation. Flag
competing assets for consolidation; pass URL and link changes to `effective-web`.

Use [Content journey and topic bank](content-journey-and-topics.md) for journey
coverage and question prompts. Include implementation or adoption questions
when they help the audience progress after purchase; do not turn every helpful
answer into a sales pitch.

## Choose distribution deliberately

Choose search discovery when people already seek the answer and a durable asset
can satisfy their intent. Choose shareable content when recognition, a useful
demonstration, or a perspective can travel through reachable communities.
Choose direct or partner distribution when access and trust matter more than
discovery. One asset may support several channels with adaptations.

Neither search nor social is a universal starting point. Match the mix to
audience behavior, current access, evidence, sales cycle, available capacity,
and time to learn. Explain the trade-off rather than prescribing a fixed ratio.
Reusing an article as several posts is worthwhile only when each version serves
its channel and reader; count useful outcomes rather than derivative volume.

## Prioritize a small actionable portfolio

Compare candidates using the dimensions that matter to this assignment:

- audience need and relevance to the chosen offer;
- evidence, distinctive expertise, and credibility available;
- distribution access and likely time to useful feedback;
- production effort, dependencies, and ongoing maintenance;
- overlap with existing assets and uncertainty the work could resolve.

State assumptions and trade-offs. Avoid unexplained universal weights or
synthetic precision. A qualitative shortlist is often enough. Keep a parking
list only when it helps the requested decision; do not inflate a small plan into
a taxonomy project.

For each selected asset, identify its audience question, primary job, angle,
format, channel, proof or research dependency, and next audience step. Add owner,
sequence, and cadence when coordination requires them. Commission the artifact
with this brief rather than including an unrequested full draft in the plan.

## Learn and maintain

Choose observations that match the asset's job: useful search visits, relevant
sharing, qualified questions, assisted decisions, successful implementation, or
another stated outcome. Separate leading signals from commercial outcomes and
acknowledge attribution limits. Set a review point that fits traffic, sales
cycle, risk, and production capacity; do not impose one cadence on all assets.

Refresh when a claim, offer, source, intent, or audience need changes, or when
observed performance warrants investigation. For commercial copy, use
[Marketing copy revision](marketing-copy-revision.md). Hand editorial refreshes
to `effective-writing` and technical causes to `effective-web`. Preserve useful
assets; retire or consolidate those that no longer earn their maintenance cost.
