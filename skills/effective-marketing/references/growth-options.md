# Growth Options

Use these as candidate mechanisms when an acquisition or distribution plan
needs alternatives. They are hypotheses to assess, not a list of proven wins.

## Start from the constraint

Name the audience, offer, current acquisition bottleneck, reachable channels,
available assets, budget, production capacity, and support capacity. Use
observed response and economics where available; state missing baselines. If
the offer or readiness is unresolved, return that decision to its owner before
increasing exposure.

## Consider mechanisms that fit

| Candidate | Why it might help | What must be checked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Useful free utility, calculator, analyzer, or limited tool | Solves a recurring task and exposes relevant people to the paid offer | Genuine need, engineering and maintenance cost, privacy, abuse, and a credible path to the paid offer |
| Template, checklist, sample data, or implementation kit | Reduces an audience's real preparation or setup effort | Expertise and accuracy, rights to material, upkeep, distribution access, and whether the audience fits |
| Importer, integration, or workflow demonstration | Reduces adoption friction or reaches an adjacent product's users | Actual compatibility, product scope, support burden, partner terms, and migration limits |
| Complementary partner or newsletter collaboration | Reaches a relevant audience through existing trust | Audience overlap, reciprocal value, permission, editorial fit, and how qualified response will be observed |
| Customer referral or advocate program | Makes a useful recommendation easier for satisfied customers | Actual satisfaction, voluntary participation, incentive cost, disclosure, and eligibility terms |
| Ecosystem directory or marketplace presence | Meets buyers where they already evaluate a workflow | Current admission rules, fees, dependency risk, accurate listing claims, and support expectations |
| Developer education, examples, or community participation | Helps technical buyers evaluate and adopt a product | A real technical audience, useful expertise, working examples, community norms, and ongoing ownership |
| Original evidence, benchmark, or research-based resource | Gives the audience a credible answer and a reason to share it | Valid method, access and consent, limits, production effort, and whether the result is useful even without a favorable headline |

Use existing channel routes for paid media, outbound, social, sales, or CRM
execution. This shortlist supplements those mechanisms; choose only candidates
that solve the stated bottleneck. Do not assume every software product needs a
free tool, community, affiliate program, or API.

## Turn the shortlist into a test

For promising options, state the mechanism, audience access, key assumption,
effort, dependencies, downside, and observation that would justify continuation.
Prefer the smallest legitimate test that distinguishes useful response from
vanity activity. For example, distribute an existing approved template before
building a tool, or test a partner placement before committing to a program.

Define success, stop, or revise conditions relative to known economics and the
learning question. When the baseline is unknown, propose an initial observation
and explain its limit rather than inventing a conversion target or promised
uplift. Include delivery and support work in cost, not just acquisition spend.

Return a ranked shortlist or selected test with its rationale, owner dependencies,
and unresolved assumptions. Use qualitative trade-offs when numbers would be
speculative; do not bury the decision under a large catalog.

## Keep authority and ownership clear

- New product scope, utility requirements, integration commitments, referral
  economics, and pricing or eligibility policy go to `effective-product`.
- Software implementation and repository-derived technical examples go to
  `effective-engineering` and `effective-delivery`; web surfaces and technical
  search implementation go to `effective-web`.
- Customer fieldwork and research methods belong to `effective-product`.
  Publishing an informative resource belongs to `effective-writing`.
- Selecting an idea does not authorize building it, spending, publishing,
  contacting a partner, enrolling customers, sharing audience data, or changing
  external accounts. Proceed when the action and destination are authorized.
