# Route: Content Strategy

Choose what to publish, for whom, where, and why. Use this route for a content
portfolio, topic clusters, search-intent analysis, cross-channel editorial
planning, production priorities, and maintenance decisions.

## Workflow

1. Name the audience, offer, desired audience progress, commercial outcome, and
   available production and distribution capacity. Use a supplied brief when it
   is enough; surface only gaps that materially change the plan.
2. Read [Content strategy](content-strategy.md) to inventory assets and evidence,
   group topics by decision and intent, select channels, and prioritize work.
3. Read [Content journey and topic bank](content-journey-and-topics.md) when the
   plan needs journey-stage coverage, buyer-question prompts, or a topic record.
4. Return a practicable plan: selected topics or assets, their audience and job,
   channel and format, evidence needed, production order, and learning or refresh
   triggers. Scale the detail to the request rather than requiring a calendar,
   new repository, or fixed number of pillars.

## Cross-links

- Customer and market evidence collection, competitive profiles, and research
  synthesis belong to `effective-product`. Use existing evidence; return gaps
  that would change the plan instead of inventing demand or customer language.
- Positioning and messaging remain the owners of the segment, promise, and
  approved claims. Planning topics does not authorize changing those decisions.
- The resulting commercial page or campaign goes to **Marketing Copywriting**;
  an informative article, essay, or newsletter goes to `effective-writing`.
- Platform-specific production goes to **Social Content** or **LinkedIn Posts**
  when needed. A content portfolio need not be a social calendar.
- Search-oriented topic selection stays here. Site architecture, internal-link
  implementation, redirects, metadata, structured data, crawling, accessibility,
  analytics implementation, and technical search diagnosis go to `effective-web`.

The plan does not by itself authorize publishing, contacting partners,
collecting personal data, or changing external systems.
