# Marketing Copy Revision

Revise in focused passes. A single line edit tends to polish the surface while
leaving the commercial argument, proof, or action path unchanged.

## Set the editing contract

Before editing, identify:

- whether the core position, message, offer, and claims are approved;
- which facts, terms, qualifications, and voice choices must remain;
- whether the user wants a critique, minimal edit, rewrite, or alternatives;
- the primary reader, entry context, and next action;
- the scope: one line, section, artifact, or cross-channel consistency.

When the core message is not approved, diagnose and return the decision to
the positioning and messaging routes instead of silently solving strategy through prose.

## Refresh an existing asset

Refresh when evidence, product behavior, terms, audience needs, or performance
suggest a substantive change. Age alone does not make content wrong.

1. Establish the current baseline: asset, intended audience and action, source
   dates, claim dependencies, and available performance evidence. A traffic or
   conversion decline can reflect measurement, technical delivery, distribution,
   seasonality, or a changed audience; inspect those explanations before
   prescribing new copy.
2. Revalidate consequential facts against current sources. Check linked prices,
   plans, screenshots, policies, comparisons, examples, and proof. Remove or
   qualify unsupported claims rather than making old evidence sound current.
3. Choose the intervention: correct a local fact; refresh examples and missing
   decision information; restructure a useful but confused argument; or rewrite
   after the positioning owner resolves a changed audience, offer, or promise.
   Consider consolidation or retirement when assets duplicate one decision or
   no longer serve an audience need. Let `effective-web` implement URL changes
   and redirects with the appropriate owner.
4. Preserve useful material and deliberate voice. Apply the passes below to the
   changed argument and its dependencies. Record what changed and why when a
   review record is useful; do not change a publication date merely to imply
   freshness. Use a truthful update date after substantive revision.
5. Choose a follow-up observation appropriate to the change and available
   traffic. Do not promise recovered rankings or attribute improvement to copy
   without an adequate comparison.

Commercial-choice copy stays here. Refreshing an informative article or
newsletter belongs to `effective-writing`; technical search and analytics
diagnosis belongs to `effective-web`.

## Run the passes

### 1. Intent and message

- Can the reader tell what this is, who it is for, and why it matters?
- Does one governing promise organize the artifact?
- Does the draft express the approved position rather than a new generic one?
- Are sections serving distinct jobs or repeating claims in new words?

### 2. Relevance and consequence

- Does each important capability answer what changes for this reader?
- Is the current situation or alternative recognizable and fair?
- Are consequences concrete without converting a plausible benefit into a
  guaranteed result?
- Can low-value company history, throat-clearing, or internal vocabulary move?

### 3. Claims and proof

- Which claims concern outcome, comparison, speed, ease, scale, universality,
  popularity, safety, savings, or superiority?
- Is appropriate proof close enough to each claim?
- Are sample, segment, period, baseline, conditions, and uncertainty preserved?
- Should an unsupported claim be narrowed, reframed as mechanism, marked for
  evidence, or removed?

### 4. Specificity and concreteness

- Replace empty intensity with observable behavior, examples, quantities, or
  conditions that are actually known.
- Prefer precise nouns and verbs to strings of abstract benefits.
- Keep approved technical terms; explain their consequence when the audience
  needs it.
- Do not insert invented numbers merely to make the writing feel specific.

### 5. Emotional truth

- Does the copy create recognition, stakes, anticipation, relief, agency, or
  confidence where the evidence supports it?
- Is emotion carried by a real moment, choice, or consequence rather than
  adjectives, fear, or synthetic storytelling?
- Does the intensity fit the offer's risk, reversibility, and relationship?
- Has a clever phrase displaced clarity or credibility?

### 6. Objections, fit, and risk

- Are the strongest legitimate alternatives and objections treated fairly?
- Are material costs, effort, eligibility, switching, dependencies, and limits
  visible before commitment?
- Does reassurance rely on real policy, proof, process, or control?
- Are refusal, cancellation, privacy, and reversibility clear where relevant?

### 7. Action

- Is the primary next step obvious and proportionate?
- Does the call to action say what happens or what the reader receives?
- Are secondary actions useful rather than competing?
- Does language around the action preserve consent and material conditions?

### 8. Voice, rhythm, and mechanics

- Preserve intentional character while removing status language, clutter, and
  accidental repetition.
- Vary sentence and paragraph shape with meaning rather than enforcing a fixed
  word count.
- Use active voice when it clarifies responsibility; keep passive voice when
  the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally backgrounded.
- Read headings and openings as a scan path, then read the whole artifact aloud
  for cadence and transitions.
- Correct grammar, spelling, punctuation, terminology, links, and formatting
  after meaning is stable.

After each consequential change, recheck earlier passes. A more emotional line
may exceed the proof; a shorter line may drop the qualification; a voice edit
may change the intended claim.

## Distinguish correction from preference

Label feedback when useful:

- **Must fix:** false, unsupported, ambiguous, inaccessible, misleading,
  contradictory, materially incomplete, or outside the brief.
- **Should fix:** weakens relevance, sequence, proof, comprehension, trust, or
  action for the intended reader.
- **Preference:** an equally valid voice, rhythm, or wording choice.

Do not use an invented panel of experts or fictional customer personas as
evidence. If multiple perspectives matter, review the copy against explicit
roles, supplied research, known accessibility needs, and actual decision
criteria.

## Final quality gate

- The artifact fulfills the assignment and matches its entry promise.
- Product, position, offer, terminology, and constraints remain accurate.
- The belief path is understandable in a scan and a full read.
- Claims have proportional, adjacent proof or honest qualification.
- Emotional resonance comes from truthful relevance, not pressure.
- The strongest objection and material risk are handled fairly.
- The primary action is clear, informed, and proportionate.
- Voice is recognizable; corrections have not flattened it into generic polish.
- No customer language, testimonial, result, urgency, scarcity, or certainty
  has been invented.
- Remaining gaps and owner decisions are visible outside the publishable copy.
