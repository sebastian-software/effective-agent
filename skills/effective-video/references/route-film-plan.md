# Film Plan

Use before producing a new film or a substantially new cut. The plan decides
length, structure, and the shared timing grid; every later route builds on it.

## Settle the Brief

Ask only for decisions that change the film and that the product, site, or
repository cannot answer:

- audience and placement: homepage hero, social feed, README, talk;
- format: one 16:9 1080p master by default; vertical and square cuts are
  separate deliverables;
- target length as a range; a 45–75 s promo carries one idea per scene;
- tone, and how much humour a hook may carry before the film turns serious;
- voice: language, accent, register ("keynote" vs. "advert"), gender if it
  matters to the requester;
- music sourcing: an original composition, a licensed track, or none.

Look up facts yourself: product capabilities, current headline copy, design
tokens, fonts, logo files, and existing animations. Reuse the product's own
visual vocabulary rather than inventing a new one for the film.

## Beat Sheet

Write the script as beats: one spoken line, one visual action, one purpose
each. A useful promo arc:

1. hook: a recognisable problem in a few seconds, concrete rather than abstract;
2. introduction: name the product and its core promise;
3. demonstration: the product doing the job, request to result;
4. scale or team value;
5. trust: what makes it safe or reliable;
6. claim and call to action.

Show the product's own mechanics in the demonstration and trust beats. Generic
kinetic typography can carry a claim, but it does not explain a product.

Budget speech at about 140–150 words per minute for a calm keynote voice,
then leave air: a 60 s film carries roughly 110–130 words. Measure the real
voice takes before fixing scene lengths.

## One Timing Grid

Choose a tempo and derive everything from bars: at 86 BPM in 4/4 a bar lasts
240 / 86 ≈ 2.79 s. Put scene boundaries on downbeats and accents on beats.
Keep the bar plan in one place that both the picture timeline and the score
generator read, or define it identically in both and change both together.

Fit voice to the grid, not the grid to the voice: place each line so its key
word lands near the visual it names, and let the last words of the claim land
on the final chord. When a line overruns a bar, extend the section by a bar
rather than compressing speech.

## Checkpoints

Stop for the requester's judgment where taste decides and rework is costly:

1. voice samples: the same two or three lines in several voices;
2. animatic: rough picture, final timing, voice on music;
3. final render with captions.

Between checkpoints, decide by convention and say so. Keep the brief, beat
sheet, and checkpoint decisions short enough to paste into a pull request.

## Variants

Build alternative hooks or cuts from the approved master: change the hook beat
and keep everything after the first shared downbeat identical, so music, voice,
and picture stay in sync without rework.
